Your routine スキンケア

Japan supplies the steps where its products beat US ones (dark-spot lotion, vitamin C serum, sunscreen) and the ones that are much cheaper there (cleanser, moisturizer). For acne, Japan has a gentle spot cream; adapalene from home is stronger. The order matters, so the steps are numbered.

Morning

  1. Water, or Curél Foaming Wash if oilyJapan, cheaper. Summer: use the wash. Winter: water is often enough.
  2. Hada Labo Shirojyun Premium lotionJapan. Tranexamic acid for dark spots.
  3. MoisturizerJapan, cheaper: Naturie gel in summer, Curél cream in winter.
  4. Japanese sunscreen, SPF50+ PA++++Japan. Biore Aqua Rich daily, Anessa on long outdoor days. Reapply midday.

Evening

  1. Curél Foaming WashJapan, cheaper.
  2. Hada Labo Shirojyun Premium lotionJapan.
  3. Melano CC Premium vitamin C serumJapan. Start every other night.
  4. MoisturizerSame as morning.
  5. Acne treatment on breakoutsPair Acne Cream W (Japan, gentle) or adapalene from home.

Start one new product at a time, a few days apart. If you use adapalene (Differin) or another retinoid from home, put it on different nights from the vitamin C serum at first. If you're pregnant or on prescription acne medicine, check with a doctor first.

Sunscreen 日焼け止め

The strongest reason to shop in Japan. Japanese sunscreens use UV filters that US products still can't use, which gives stronger UVA protection (the rays behind dark spots) in a much lighter, invisible texture. The FDA approved its first new filter, bemotrizinol, in June 2026, but US products with it aren't widely sold yet, and filters like Uvinul A Plus still aren't allowed. Look for PA++++ on the box: it's Japan's top UVA rating, which US labels don't show.

Tinted, for melasma & dark marks

Visible light also darkens melasma and post-acne marks, especially on medium and darker skin, and clear sunscreens let most of it through. Iron oxides, the pigments in tinted sunscreens, block it. In trials, tinted iron-oxide sunscreens improved melasma more and reduced relapses compared with UV-only ones. Wear one as your last step, either on its own or on top of a clear sunscreen such as Biore UV Aqua Rich or Minon UV Mild Milk. Let the sunscreen dry for a minute first. Use about two finger-lengths for your face, and test the shade on your jaw.

How to test fairly

  • Change one product at a time and keep the rest of your routine the same.
  • Dark-spot products need 8–12 weeks. Sunscreens need 3–4 weeks to tell whether they clog pores.
  • Patch-test along your jaw for 2–3 nights first.
  • Take a photo each week in the same light.
  • Buy one of each alternative first, and stock up only on what works.

Hair color ヘアカラー

Japan's foam dyes are the easiest home dyes to use: you work them in like shampoo, so there are no missed patches at the back. There are two kinds. Permanent foam dyes change color in one go but use oxidation dyes (PPD, called ジアミン), the main cause of hair-dye allergy. Color treatments (カラートリートメント) skip those dyes and are much gentler on sensitive skin, but they add color gradually and can't lighten hair.

Before you dye

  • Patch test every time with permanent dyes, 48 hours before, even with a brand you've used before. PPD allergy can start after years of problem-free use. If you've ever reacted to hair dye or a black henna tattoo, stick to color treatments.
  • Box photos show dark Asian hair. On lighter or previously colored hair, results come out lighter or ashier. Do a strand test first.
  • Pastel and vivid shades (Palty, Freshlight) need bleached hair first, so they're not a good home project.
  • Pack dyes in your checked bag. The developer is a liquid.

Expiry rules in Japan

  • Most cosmetics have no printed dateJapanese law only requires an expiry date when a product stays stable for less than 3 years unopened. No date means it's designed to last at least 3 years sealed, stored cool and dry. Shop stock may already be a year old, so plan to use everything within about 2 years.
  • Medicines do have a dateMuhi prints 使用期限 (use by) on the box. Check it before you pay.
  • The clock starts when you open itLotions and serums: about 3 months once opened. Sunscreen: 3–6 months. Open one at a time and keep the rest sealed.
  • Don't overbuy sunscreen or vitamin CSunscreen ranges are refreshed every spring and are best used within about a year. Vitamin C is the most fragile ingredient here. The planner caps both at 12 months.
  • Store it coolAbout 15–25°C, out of sunlight.

Shopping tips 免税

  • Tax-free changes on November 1, 2026Before that date, the 10% tax comes off at the register. From November 1 you pay full price and claim the refund at an airport kiosk when you leave. You need ¥5,000+ before tax at one store on one day, and your passport. Keep tax-free items in your carry-on, and don't use them before you leave or you lose the refund.
  • Where to shopMatsumoto Kiyoshi, Sundrug, Welcia and Don Quijote stock everything here. Discount chains like COSMOS are often 10–20% cheaper.
  • Showing staffShow the Japanese name under each product.
  • Check it's the Japanese versionSome of these are also sold in the US under the same name but a different formula. Japanese text and PA++++ on the box confirm the Japanese version.
