More drugstore finds ドラッグストア

Gaps the main list didn't cover: dry winter patches, whiteheads, chapped hands and cold days. All sold at Matsukiyo and Sundrug.

    Skip Japanese cold and cough medicines such as Pabron or Lulu if the box lists ジヒドロコデイン (dihydrocodeine). It's a codeine-type ingredient that's a controlled substance in the US. Buy cold medicine at home instead.

    Food to bring home 食品

    Shelf-stable, light, and free of meat, so they're the kind US customs lets through. Declare all food on your customs form anyway. Liquids and pastes go in your checked bag.

      From the supermarket

      Cheap everyday staples that are hard to find, pricier or a different formula at home. OK Store Ginza is the cheapest option on your route; Seijo Ishii by your hotel is pricier but easy. Bottles go in your checked bag, wrapped in a plastic bag.

        Check the label before you buy: skip anything listing 鶏がら, チキンエキス, ポークエキス or 牛脂 (chicken, pork or beef stock or fat). That rules out most curry roux, many white dashi (Mizkan 白だし contains chicken stock), and the eggplant flavor in Amano's miso soup variety pack. Also skip Kewpie mayonnaise: it's made with raw egg, so it doesn't count as cooked egg at US customs.

        Snacks: Japan-only KitKat flavors, Jagabee, Pocky and Hi-Chew are cheapest at Don Quijote and supermarkets, so grab them in Shinjuku tonight. They're sealed sweets, which customs generally accepts. Pantry browsing: AKOMEYA TOKYO in Ginza is a curated Japanese pantry store (rice, seasonings, kitchen tools) if you have time on Sunday. I couldn't confirm its current Ginza address and hours.

            US customs for food 税関

            • Declare all foodTick "yes" for food on your customs form and list it. Undeclared food is usually taken away, and you can be fined. Declared items that aren't allowed are just confiscated.
            • Skip meat, including meat extractsPork and beef products from Japan are restricted. Most Japanese curry roux and instant ramen contain pork or chicken extract (ポークエキス, チキンエキス) and are often confiscated. Check the ingredient list.
            • Egg is fine when it's cooked and shelf-stableUSDA allows processed egg products from Japan for personal use if they're commercially cooked and sealed, like the egg in furikake.
            • No fresh produceFresh fruit and vegetables aren't allowed. Dried, sealed and packaged foods, tea, and sealed sweets are generally fine.
            • Skip refrigerated itemsMentaiko, fresh mochi and chilled sweets won't survive the flight anyway.
